When to use a warning text

Use the warning text component when you need to warn users about something important, such as legal consequences of an action, or lack of action, that they might take.

When not to use a warning text

Do not use a warning text when:

  • the information is helpful but not critical — use body text, inset text or a hint instead
  • you're describing routine risks that people already expect or understand
  • the consequence is minor, reversible or low-impact
  • you need to highlight an error — use an error summary or error status instead
  • repeating warnings across the page would dilute their impact or overwhelm the user

How to use a warning text

To use warning text effectively:

  • place it close to the action or decision it relates to so users see it at the right moment
  • state the risk clearly and concisely — say what might happen and why it matters
  • keep the message brief and focused on the key consequence
  • test the placement and wording with users to ensure the warning is noticed and understood

Accessibility

Do not rely on colour alone to communicate a warning. Use text and icons to ensure all users can recognise important information. This supports WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.1 Use of Colour, level A.

Ensure warning icons and visual indicators have sufficient contrast against surrounding colours. This supports WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.11: Non-text Contrast, level AA.

Include hidden text such as "Warning" so assistive technologies can identify the content as a warning. You might need to rewrite the hidden text to make it appropriate for your context. This supports WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.3.1: Info and Relationships, level A.
