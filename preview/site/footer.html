When to use the footer

Use the public-facing footer on websites and services used by members of the public. It helps users recognise they are on an NHS Wales service and provides links they expect to find at the bottom of a page. Do not use this footer in mobile apps.

For internal-facing services, use the footer designed for services that are not hosted on a service.nhs.wales domain.

If you use the page template, the footer is included automatically and does not need to be added separately. To customise the default footer, read the page template guidance about customising components.

How to use the footer

Adding links

You can add links to:

  • your service's privacy notice
  • your service's accessibility statement
  • your service's cookies page
  • terms and conditions
  • help and support content

Use 'Privacy', 'Accessibility', 'Cookies' and 'Terms and conditions' for the link text.

! Warning callout

Do not use generic content for these pages. You must make sure the content reflects the specific needs, users and context of your service.

If you include links to 'help' pages within the footer component, make sure to place those links consistently within the footer content.

Keep links to a minimum, especially in a transactional journey. Too many links can distract users. Link to specific policies for the transaction, if appropriate.

If your total number of links is less than 7, arrange them horizontally in lines.

Copyright

Your copyright statement must reflect the ownership of your website or service.

Include a reference to the Open Government Licence if your organisation or service encourages people to reuse and adapt its information under the licence. Contact your legal team for advice.

Accessibility

Make sure that users can successfully find help links in a consistent place on each page.

If you include links to "help" pages in a footer, make sure to place those links consistently in the footer content. Also make sure that "help" links always work in a similar way across each page. This supports WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 3.2.6 Consistent Help, level A.

Source

Learn more about Footers in the NHS England Design System.

Follow discussions about Footers on NHS England GitHub to see the latest updates and resolved issues.

Footer with navigation links

If your users need lots of footer links to help them navigate a complex information architecture, include navigation links and arrange the links in columns.

The navigation links give users another route to the most visited content on the site. They act as a safety net for users who have not found content through the main navigation.

There is a line below the columns to separate them from the meta information, for example the copyright notice.

Footer with an open licence

If your organisation or service encourages people to reuse and adapt its information under the Open Government Licence, show the licence statement below the links and above the copyright line, each as its own row.
