What you’ll do

Publish your prototype so other people can open it in a web browser.

Before you publish

You must:

  • use fictional or test information, never real patient or identifiable personal information
  • check your prototype does not contain any sensitive information before you share it
  • add a phase banner to every page to make it clear that this is a prototype, not a live service
  • check with your digital team which hosting service you should use

Publish your prototype

Store your prototype in GitHub

Before you publish your prototype, make sure your prototype is stored in GitHub.

Hosting services can connect to your GitHub repository to publish your prototype and update it when you push changes.

Choose a hosting service

The prototype kit needs a hosting service that supports Node.js. It will not work with static hosting services such as GitHub Pages.

Check with your team or technical lead which hosting service you should use.

DHCW has tested the prototype kit with Render. You can use a different hosting service if your team already has one.

Publish with Render

Follow the Deploying on Render guidance to publish your prototype.

Once published, your prototype will have a web address that you can share with people who need to view or test it.

Configure your prototype

Set environment variables

You may need to set environment variables when you publish your prototype.

Check your hosting service’s guidance for how to do this.

If you’re using Render, read Environment Variables and Secrets. Render lets you manage these from the Environment section of your service.

Set a password

If you publish your prototype online, protect it with a password. This helps prevent people finding the prototype and mistaking it for a live service.

Set the PROTOTYPE_PASSWORD environment variable to the password you want to use.

Run your prototype in production mode

When you publish your prototype, set the NODE_ENV variable to production .

Your hosting service may set this for you. Check its documentation before adding it yourself.

Share your prototype

Once your prototype is published:

  1. Open it and check it works.
  2. Check it is clearly marked as a prototype, for example by using a phase banner.
  3. Copy the web address.
  4. Share the web address with the people who need to view or test it.
